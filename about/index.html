About

A writer who would rather be understood than admired.

Studio Alder is a one-person practice in the north of England. I write for people who make things properly and want to talk about them the same way.

I grew up above my grandmother's stationery shop, which is probably where it started. She believed a good letter could fix most things, and that a bad one was worse than silence. I have been trying to live up to that ever since.

After a decade as a magazine editor in London, I moved back north in 2019 and opened the studio. The work is varied on purpose. One month it is a long essay about a boatbuilder; the next it is every word on a bakery's website, down to the label on the flour sacks.

How I work

Every project begins with a visit. I ask a lot of questions, most of them simple. Why this way and not the easier way? What do your regulars call it? What would you never say? The answers are usually better than anything I could invent, so my job is to notice them and put them in the right order.

Then I write, slowly, and cut, quickly. I read everything aloud before anyone else sees it. If a sentence makes me run out of breath, it goes.

What I believe

Short words are stronger than long ones. Specific is kinder than clever. Readers are busy and bright, and they can tell when they are being sold to. Respect them and they will stay to the end.

I take on a small number of projects each year so that each one gets my full attention. If that sounds like the kind of help you need, I would like to hear from you.
